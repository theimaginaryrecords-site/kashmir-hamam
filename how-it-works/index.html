The Craft

How a Kashmiri Hamam Works

No wires. No bills. Just wood, stone and a design perfected over generations.

1 · The Firebox

Where the Heat Begins

A small wood-fired chamber — the firebox — sits at one end of the room, usually below floor level. You light firewood in it morning and evening, just like a traditional bukhari, but the heat does not escape into the air. Instead it is pulled under the floor.

2 · The Hollow Floor

Heat Travels Under Your Feet

The floor is built hollow: a network of brick channels runs beneath the entire room. Hot air and smoke from the firebox flow through these channels, warming everything above them evenly — no cold corners — before exiting through the chimney.

3 · The Stone Slabs

Stone That Holds Warmth for a Day

Over the channels lie thick slabs of original Devar stone. Dense and slow to heat, the stone soaks up warmth and releases it gently for 24 to 36 hours after a single firing. The floor stays warm through the night and into the next day.

4 · The Copper Trei

Hot Water as a Bonus

Beside the firebox sits the trei — a hand-hammered copper water tank. It catches waste heat from the firebox, so the same fire that warms your floor also gives you hot water for washing, without a geyser.

Heritage

A Kashmiri Tradition

The hamam came to Kashmir centuries ago and became part of how we survive winter — in homes, in mosques, in the old town houses of Srinagar. On the coldest nights, when the power fails and the chillai kalan bites hardest, the hamam floor is still warm.

We build them the way our elders did: real Devar stone, honest brickwork, copper beaten by hand. No shortcuts, no duplicate stone, no compromises — and we stand behind every floor with a lifetime guarantee.
